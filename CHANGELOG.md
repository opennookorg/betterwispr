# Changelog

All notable changes to BetterWispr are listed here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the project uses
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). The current version lives in `VERSION`.

## [0.1.6] - 2026-10-15

### Added
- Transcribe audio and video files and web links from the new Transcribe page in the dashboard. Drop or choose an MP3, M4A, AAC, WAV, AIFF, CAF, FLAC, MP4, MOV or M4V file, or paste a link such as a YouTube video. BetterWispr transcribes it on this Mac with the model you pick on the page, or with an API connection only if you pick one. Links download with yt-dlp, a free open-source downloader. Nothing downloads until you click Install yt-dlp, and BetterWispr checks it against its published checksum first. YouTube links work best with Deno installed (`brew install deno`). Long recordings are cut at the quietest moment every 30 to 60 seconds. On three TED videos, cutting this way got 1,233 words wrong out of 12,592, against 1,282 when sending the whole file at once and 1,335 when cutting it like meeting audio. Transcripts keep their timestamps, can be copied or exported as plain text or Markdown, and label Speaker 1, Speaker 2 when Speaker detection is installed. `BetterWisprCLI --media PATH|URL` does the same from the command line, and `--download-yt-dlp` installs yt-dlp.
- A built-in notes model that runs on this Mac. Download Qwen3.5 2B (1.28 GB) under Meeting notes in Models, and it writes meeting notes, edits Medium dictation and answers Ask without Apple Intelligence, Ollama, a CLI or an API key. It supports macOS 14. It uses about 1.5 GB of memory while it works, most of it the model file read from disk, and frees it after five idle minutes. BetterWispr uses it only when you choose it, and it never downloads on its own. A finished download makes it your notes provider unless you picked another one meanwhile. A 2B model can repeat points or give a task to the wrong person, so check its notes. `BetterWisprCLI --download-builtin-model` installs it, and `--notes-model builtin` uses it with `--meeting` and `--bench`.
- Ask questions about one meeting from its Ask tab, or about all your meetings from the Notetaker page. Answers cite the transcript lines they came from, and clicking a citation opens the meeting at that line. Ask sends your question and the meeting lines that best match it to the notes provider you pick for Ask, or to the one that writes your summaries when you keep Same as notes. A meeting that is still being processed can be asked about from its transcript so far.
- Ask keeps your questions and answers. A meeting's Ask tab shows the ones about that meeting, and the Notetaker page shows the ones about all your meetings, after you quit and reopen BetterWispr. Each answer names the model that wrote it. Clear removes them from this Mac after you confirm. Deleting a meeting also removes the answers about all meetings that cite it. A question that fails or is cancelled isn't kept.
- What did I miss? in the Ask tab of a meeting that is recording. It answers from the last five minutes of the transcript, with citations, and leaves out My thoughts.
- Filters for Ask on the Notetaker page. Ask about meetings from today, the past 7 days or the past 30 days, and about meetings with one Calendar invitee.
- Provider presets for a new notes API connection in Models. Pick OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini or OpenRouter to fill in its address and a fast model, then add your API key. Anthropic says its OpenAI-compatible API is not a long-term or production-ready solution.
- Calendar for meeting notes, in Settings > Calendar and off by default. Turning it on asks macOS for Calendar access. A note started during an event, or up to five minutes before it, takes the event's title and lists the people invited under its date. BetterWispr reads the calendars already in the Calendar app on this Mac, from every account including shared and subscribed calendars, so it works offline and needs no sign-in. Each calendar has its own switch, and calendars added later are included. Declined and cancelled events are skipped. When events overlap, the one with a Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams or Webex link wins. The event title and invitees go to your notes model with the transcript. Invitee names also help the speech model recognize them.
- Connected apps, in Settings and off by default. Turn on Share meetings with AI apps, and Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor and other apps that support MCP can list and search your meetings and read their summaries, action items, your notes and transcripts with speaker names and timestamps. Settings shows the line to run for Claude Code, the snippet to paste for Claude Desktop and Cursor, and the command for other apps, each with a Copy button. Connected apps can only read, never change or delete anything. BetterWispr checks the setting on every request, so turning it off stops sharing right away without restarting the other app. BetterWispr itself sends nothing over the network, but an app you connect sends what it reads to its AI provider. Long meetings arrive in parts, and files from the Transcribe page are not shared.
- Correct the speakers after a meeting. Click the speaker name on a line from the other side of the call to give it to another speaker or a new one, or use the menu beside a speaker's name field to merge that speaker into another. A merged speaker keeps its target's name, or takes the other name when the target has none. A merge can't be undone, but you can move lines back one at a time. The speaker menus appear once the meeting has finished processing, and your own lines stay Me.
- A meeting recorded without Speaker detection offers to download it. The download applies from the next meeting, and you can dismiss the offer for good.
- Press Escape to cancel a dictation from any app while it is starting, recording or transcribing. Escape also works with your shortcut's Option, Control or Shift still held, but never with Command, so Command-Option-Escape stays Force Quit. Undo still appears when audio was captured. Escape goes back to the app you're in as soon as the dictation ends. Hovering the capsule while you dictate shows "Esc to cancel".
- An optional second shortcut for cancelling a dictation, in Settings > Shortcut. It is off by default and Escape keeps working. Record a combination with a key, such as ⌃⌥K or F5, and it cancels from any app while a dictation is starting, recording or transcribing, without pasting it. A modifier key alone can't be used, and neither can your dictation shortcut. The combination works as usual in other apps whenever you aren't dictating. Clear turns it off.
- Fn (🌐) can be the dictation shortcut on its own. In Settings, click the keyboard shortcut and press and release Fn. Like other single modifier keys it needs Accessibility, and it only starts dictation when no other modifier is held. Set "Press 🌐 key to" to "Do Nothing" in System Settings > Keyboard so macOS doesn't also show emoji or switch the input source.
- Pause music while dictating. A new setting pauses Music and Spotify while the microphone is open for a dictation and resumes only what it paused when you stop or cancel. It is off by default and asks macOS for permission to control those apps when you turn it on.
- A start sound plays when the capsule begins listening, so you know the microphone is open before you speak. It is the macOS Tink sound, and a `started.wav` file in `~/Library/Application Support/BetterWispr/SoundOverrides` replaces it. It stays quiet when a dictation fails to start, while a meeting is being recorded, and with Dictation sounds off.
- Choose each dictation sound in Settings > App. With Dictation sounds on, Start, Finish, Cancel and Needs attention each list the macOS system sounds, and picking one plays it. A sound you don't pick stays Tink, Purr, Bottle or Basso. A file in `SoundOverrides` still wins over the picker, and that picker says so and is greyed out. Previews stay quiet while a meeting is being recorded.
- Hide capsule while dictating, in Settings > App and off by default. The capsule stays out of sight while you speak and while your words are transcribed. Errors, Undo and the copied and finished cards still appear, and the capsule comes back for the last 30 seconds before a long dictation stops. Escape and your cancel shortcut still cancel, and the menu bar icon turns into a microphone while you speak. Show words as you speak is off while the capsule is hidden.
- A Cream theme, in Settings > Appearance, for a warmer page than Light's pure white. It paints the dashboard, the welcome guide and the Notetaker panel cream with slightly lighter cards, and keeps the system text colors and controls. Body text passes WCAG AA contrast. Grey secondary text is about 3.9:1, the same as in Light.
- Unload speech model when idle, in Settings > Microphone and language. Choose After 5 minutes, After 15 minutes or After 1 hour to free the speech model's memory between dictations. The next dictation loads the model again from disk, so it takes a moment longer to finish. It is Never by default, the timer never runs out during a dictation, and waking or unlocking the Mac doesn't load a freed model back. The built-in notes model keeps its own five-minute unload.
- Each speech model in Models lists the languages it supports by name, in your Mac's language. A model with one language shows its name, and a model with more shows a count, such as "25 languages", that opens to the full list.
- Dictation languages, in Settings > Microphone and language, shows with a Whisper model and Spoken language set to Detect automatically. Pick the languages you speak, such as English and Spanish, and detection chooses the most likely of them instead of any language Whisper knows. With languages picked, the first 30 seconds of a dictation decide its language, which takes one more pass over that audio before transcribing. All languages, the default, detects as before. Other models ignore the list. `BetterWisprCLI --transcribe-file PATH --languages en,es` does the same.
- Chinese script, in Settings > Microphone and language. Choose Simplified or Traditional to get Chinese dictation in those characters, whichever script the speech model wrote. It shows when Spoken language is Chinese, or Detect automatically with a model that supports Chinese, where it converts only dictations recognized as Chinese, so Japanese stays as it is. The conversion uses the converter built into macOS and runs on this Mac. As recognized, the default, leaves the characters alone, and History's Original transcription keeps what the model wrote.
- The dashboard sidebar shows the app version with a What's new link to the changelog. About has new Changelog and Support rows, and Support opens Buy Me a Coffee.

### Changed
- A dictation can now run for ten minutes instead of two. Before, it stopped at two minutes without warning. In the last 30 seconds the capsule counts down with "Stops in 0:30", and VoiceOver says "Dictation stops in 30 seconds." Apple on-device recognition, the Parakeet live preview and API connections all handle the full ten minutes. An API connection gets a dictation longer than about two minutes in parts, which can cut a word where two parts meet.
- With a Whisper model selected, Spoken language in Settings offers every language that model supports, about 100, instead of the shorter list shared with the other models.
- Speaker detection now works on macOS 14. It still needs the call audio the notetaker records on macOS 14.2 or later. It uses new models from Argmax SpeakerKit, about 15 MB, in place of the ones 0.1.5 downloaded. If you installed Speaker detection in 0.1.5, Models shows it as not downloaded, so download it again under Meeting speakers. Once the new download finishes, BetterWispr removes the 0.1.5 files, about 21 MB. Meetings you already recorded keep their speaker labels and names. The speaker accuracy figures in 0.1.5 were measured with the old models and have not been measured again with the new ones.
- Use screen context now also fixes misspelled names in a dictation with the names in the window you dictate into, such as a teammate's name in a chat. It works at every cleanup level, so its switches now show without Medium. It needs Parakeet with the phrase booster and English or automatic language. Text read for spelling stays on this Mac. Only Medium cleanup sends screen context to your notes model, as before.
- Start the next meeting while the last one is still processing. Stopping a meeting no longer locks the notetaker while it transcribes its last chunks, finds speakers and writes notes. The capsule, the menu bar and the dashboard can start a new meeting right away, and the stopped one finishes on its own. Its last words stay in its own transcript, and the dashboard does not jump to its note while the new meeting records. Skip, Cancel and Update summary act on one meeting. While two meetings overlap, each holds its own copy of the speech model.

### Fixed
- Long meetings no longer fail with "The model couldn't condense this meeting enough". BetterWispr shrinks a long transcript in up to 8 rounds instead of 3, asks for fewer words when the notes model writes more than it was asked for, and when the model still can't shrink the meeting enough, writes notes for each remaining part and joins them in order. Such notes can be long and repeat a point, but every part of the meeting goes into them. The Summary tab also lets you pick another notes model next to Generate summary, so you can retry without going to Models.
- Dictation sounds now play on a new install. With the setting on, BetterWispr stayed silent unless you had added your own sound files, because none ship with the app. Finishing a dictation now plays the macOS Purr sound, cancelling plays Bottle, and a problem that needs your attention plays Basso. A `finished.wav`, `cancelled.wav` or `attention.wav` file in `~/Library/Application Support/BetterWispr/SoundOverrides` still replaces the matching sound.
- Claude Code and Codex are now found when installed through nix or anywhere your login shell can run them, so Meeting notes and Medium cleanup no longer report them as missing.
- Tagalog dictation with Smallest AI no longer fails as an unsupported language. BetterWispr renamed the language's code on the way out, and Smallest AI accepts only the original. Javanese and Norwegian are now sent the same way, with the codes Whisper uses.

## [0.1.5] - 2026-10-12

### Added
- Meeting notes can tell the people on the other side of a call apart. Download Speaker detection (about 21 MB) under Meeting speakers in Models. When a meeting ends, BetterWispr listens back to the call audio on this Mac and labels each line Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on, in the order they first spoke. The notes model gets the same labels, so the summary can say who said what. While it works, the notetaker shows "Telling the speakers on the call apart" with a Skip button that goes straight to the notes. It needs macOS 15 or later and the call audio the notetaker records on macOS 14.2 or later. When the voice changes partway through a line, BetterWispr cuts the line there and transcribes each part again, so a quick reply goes to the person who gave it. On six recorded two-person calls, 93% of words went to the right person, against 85% without the cut, and on four recorded four-person meetings 82% against 77%. The shorter parts cost a little accuracy, about 4 more wrong words in every 1,000, and with an API speech connection each part is sent again. Turns shorter than a second stay with the line around them, and when two people talk at once the part goes to whoever spoke longer. Your microphone is still Me, so people in the room with you are not separated. Meetings recorded before the download keep Them.
- Name the speakers. Fields above the transcript let you type a name for each detected speaker, and the name replaces "Speaker 2" in the transcript, in Copy and Copy transcript, and in notes you generate after that. Renaming marks the summary as out of date so you can regenerate it with the names.
- `BetterWisprCLI --download-speaker-detection` installs the speaker detection models. `--meeting PATH` plays an audio file through the notetaker's chunking, transcribes it as a meeting does and prints each line with its speaker as JSON, and `--meeting LINES.jsonl --notes OUT.md` writes notes from an earlier run without transcribing again. `scripts/fetch_bench_corpus.py --meetings` downloads four recorded AMI meetings and six EdAcc video calls with who spoke when, and `scripts/evaluate_meetings.py` scores the words and the speaker labels.
- Writing modes, under Style > Modes. A mode sets its own cleanup, tone and screen context, and with Medium cleanup it can add instructions for your notes model, such as "Use British spelling." Anything left as "Same as default" follows your defaults. A mode applies in the apps you add, on the websites you add, or when you start or end a dictation with its trigger word, which BetterWispr removes from the text. With the trigger "casual", "Let's keep it casual." types "Let's keep it.", so pick a phrase you wouldn't otherwise say, such as "casual mode". A trigger word wins over a website, and a website wins over an app. A trigger word can turn screen context off but not on.
- Website modes match a site and its subdomains, plus an optional path. `github.com` also covers gist.github.com but not notgithub.com, and `*.github.com` and `github.com/apple/*` work too. They work in Safari, Chrome, Arc, Dia, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, Comet, Orion and Yandex Browser, not Firefox. BetterWispr reads the address through Accessibility when you start dictating, so there is no Automation prompt, and it never saves or sends it. While an enabled mode lists a website, the capsule can take up to half a second longer to show recording in a browser, and no audio is lost.
- Use screen context, under Style, off by default. With Medium cleanup it sends your selected text, your clipboard and the text of the window you dictate into to your notes model, so it can spell what you see. Your notes model may be a cloud service. Nothing is read while a password field is active, and clipboard items marked as passwords are skipped. Read window text from the screen, also off by default, reads the window from a screenshot when an app shares under 200 characters through Accessibility. It needs Screen Recording, and dictation never asks for it.
- Show words as you speak, in Settings > App and on by default. With a Parakeet model the capsule shows your words while you record, confirmed words bright and the newest guess dimmed, two lines at a time. The pasted text is still the recognition of the whole recording, and the preview never reaches History or correction learning.
- `BetterWisprCLI --live-file PATH [--model ID] [--language en]` plays an audio file through the live preview in real time with a Parakeet model, prints each confirmed and provisional update with the time its pass took, then prints the full transcription.
- Spoken language in Settings offers Turkish, for Apple on-device, Whisper and OpenAI-compatible connections. Parakeet models do not list it, and Sarvam and Smallest AI do not support it.
- Show in Dock, in Settings > App and off by default. BetterWispr runs from the menu bar without a Dock icon: your dictation shortcut still works, and the menu bar icon opens the dashboard or quits. Turn it on to bring the Dock icon back. The change applies right away and stays after a restart.

### Changed
- Parakeet Ultra is now the recommended model in setup and is listed above Parakeet TDT v3 in Models. Before cleanup, on 300 recorded video-call segments in Indian, Nigerian and US English, it got 14.45% of words wrong against 15.96% for Parakeet TDT v3 (14.95% against 15.66% after Light cleanup), at the same speed, with the same 25 languages. On 597 more Indian English segments it got 11.5% wrong against 12.3% before cleanup, but after Light cleanup the two were level at 12.05% and 12.11%, because cleanup removes more of the fillers v3 writes. On four recorded meetings cut into chunks as in 0.1.4, it got 22.8% wrong against 25.7%. With this version's cut at voice changes Ultra got 23.2%, and v3 was not run that way. Dictation and meeting notes both use it once selected. The model you use now does not change on its own. If you use Parakeet TDT v3 or v2, Home suggests trying Parakeet Ultra and Models marks it Recommended; download it in Models and choose it to switch.
- Learn from my corrections asks your notes model first. With Medium cleanup, English text and a ready notes model, each fix the rules would learn goes to the model with up to three words on each side, and only the fixes it confirms are learned. The model is asked to turn down everyday word swaps such as "meet" to "meat", while a misheard name such as "post hog" to "PostHog" stays allowed. If the model gives an invalid reply or isn't running, BetterWispr learns by its rules as before. The "Learned" toast has an Undo button that removes the words it just added.
- Medium cleanup uses a new edit prompt. Vocabulary is given as the spelling to use, and a mode's instructions go with it. With llama3.1:8b through Ollama, 18 of 18 test edits came back as plain edits, in 0.55 to 1.86 seconds. With Apple Intelligence, lists come back numbered and "no wait" and "sorry I mean" are resolved, but each dictation takes 0.5 to 0.8 seconds longer, and "actually scratch that" stayed in the text in 2 of 2 runs, where the old prompt removed it.
- When a Medium edit times out or hits a passing network error, BetterWispr tries up to three times, allowing 7 seconds plus 1 for every 10 words each time. Notes CLIs get one try of 10 seconds plus 1 for every 10 words. If every try fails, the dictation keeps Light cleanup.
- With Ollama, BetterWispr loads the notes model 450 ms into a recording, at the size the edit needs. When the model wasn't loaded yet, the wait before an edit fell from 0.91 to 0.06 seconds with llama3.1:8b. Apple Intelligence is prepared the same way. Dictation edits ask known API services for low reasoning effort, Claude Code gets `--effort low` and Codex gets `model_reasoning_effort="low"`.
- The local speech model runs a one-second practice transcription of quiet noise after it loads, after you switch models and 3 seconds after wake or unlock. A dictation started meanwhile cancels it. On the Mac it was tested on, the gain was within measurement noise.
- `make ship` packages DMGs with macOS's `diskutil image` instead of the `create-dmg` tool, so it no longer prints `hdiutil` deprecation warnings on macOS 27 and no longer needs `brew install create-dmg`. The disk image is APFS instead of HFS+ and keeps the same window layout. Packaging DMGs now needs macOS 27 or later; the DMGs still open on macOS 14.

### Fixed
- Light cleanup now turns "I live in Delhi no sorry I live in Ahmedabad" into "I live in Ahmedabad". A "no sorry" or "sorry, no" counts as a correction even when the speech model writes no commas, and a correction can restart on "I" or "we" when the word after it repeats too. "Should we go, no, we should stay" and "I said no sorry I can't come" stay as you said them.
- Light cleanup no longer deletes words when a "no" or "I mean" only looks like a correction. "We read it in the last book, no, the whole thing with the houses was different" lost "in the last book". A correction now has to end about as soon as the words it replaces, as "Let's meet at 5, no, at 6 pm" does.
- Light cleanup keeps repeats said for emphasis. "Drinking more and more and more" lost its last "and more", and "again and again and again" lost one "and again", as if they were stutters. "And then and then we shipped it" still becomes "and then we shipped it".
- The Parakeet phrase booster no longer replaces correctly spelled words with a vocabulary term that sounds like them. With Claude in Vocabulary it wrote "Claude" when you said "cloud", and on 597 recorded Indian English segments with 20 terms nobody said, it made 9 of those segments worse with Parakeet TDT v3 and 10 with Parakeet Ultra. A word that is spelled correctly and looks clearly different from every term now stays, which made none of them worse, while a misheard name such as "Rivali" still becomes "Diwali". Spelling is checked with the macOS English dictionary.
- Meeting notes stick to what was said. The notes model listed things that already happened in the meeting, such as introductions, as action items, gave Me tasks Me never took on, and wrote "No explicit decisions were made" as a decision. Notes now count only what someone agreed or took on, and drop items that only say there were none, while keeping real ones such as "None of the current vendors will be renewed". On seven recorded meetings, three notes each, an AI judge reading the transcripts found 1.3 unsupported claims per set of notes against 4.1 before, and 0.2 filler items against 1.4.
- Long meetings no longer fail with "The model couldn't condense this meeting enough" on Apple Intelligence. Asked to shorten the transcript, the model copied its lines back, so it never got shorter; 2 of 3 runs on a 10-minute four-person meeting failed. It now writes a short summary of each part in its own words.
- Learn from my corrections now notices a fix even when you also changed text elsewhere in the same field, and in web fields that report spaces and line breaks differently from the pasted text. Before, either case made it miss the fix.
- Fixing a dictation in History no longer moves the cursor to the end after every key you press. Text typed in the middle now goes where the cursor is.

## [0.1.4] - 2026-10-10

### Added
- English dictation writes spoken numbers as digits with every speech model, including Parakeet models that write numbers as words. "Two hundred and fifty thousand" becomes 250,000, "one hundred or two hundred" becomes "100 or 200", "two point five million" becomes "2.5 million" and "zero point one point one" becomes 0.1.1. A lone small number ("give me five minutes"), "a hundred times", ordinals, years said as pairs ("nineteen ninety nine") and digit-by-digit phone numbers stay as spoken.
- Spoken numbers understand Indian English "lakh" and "crore": "one lakh" becomes "1 lakh", "two lakh fifty thousand" becomes 2,50,000 and "two point five crore" becomes "2.5 crore". "Twelve hundred" becomes 1200, and a phrase that is not one number keeps its readable part as digits, so "ten thousand hundred followers" becomes "10,000 hundred followers".
- English dictation writes spoken symbols. "At the rate" or "at sign" writes @ ("ping at the rate KV" becomes "ping @KV", "name at the rate gmail dot com" becomes name@gmail.com), "hashtag launch" becomes #launch, "issue hash forty two" becomes "issue #42" and "ten percent" or "ten percentage" becomes 10%. Saying only "at the rate", "hash", "hashtag", "percent" or "percentage" writes just the symbol. Phrases like "hash out a plan" and "what percentage of users" stay as words. An address the model hears as "gmail. com" or "at the rategmail.com" is joined back into one address.
- Vocabulary can fix a voice command the speech model mishears in your accent. Add what it wrote as the spoken phrase and the command as "Write as", such as "Kocia Mark" written as "question mark", "Coma" as "comma", "at the Red" as "at the rate" or "Hai fun" as "hyphen". These entries run before voice commands, so the dictation gets "?", a comma, "@" or "-". They are not sent to the phrase booster, because boosting command words dropped neighbouring words in testing.
- Settings has a Vocabulary row under Microphone and language that explains how your word list helps and opens it. With a Parakeet model it tells you when the phrase booster isn't downloaded yet, since Parakeet needs it to use your words while recognizing speech.
- When a call starts in Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, a Slack huddle, Webex, FaceTime, Discord, WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, Whereby or Jitsi Meet, the pill offers Start notetaker. It asks once per call and the offer goes away after 30 seconds. Calls in a browser are recognized by their window title, which needs Accessibility. Turn it off with Detect meetings in Settings or from the offer's menu. It needs macOS 14.2 or later.
- Settings has an Appearance section with a Theme choice of System, Light or Dark. BetterWispr now starts in Dark; choose System to follow your Mac as before.
- "Exclamatory mark" writes "!", and saying only "hyphen", "colon" or "dash" writes just that mark.
- Say "write an email to Sarah saying I will be on holiday tomorrow, thanks, best regards, Kartik" and BetterWispr types it laid out as an email: "Hi Sarah," then the body, then "Best regards," and your name on their own lines. "Draft" and "compose" work too, as do "that", "telling her" and "letting him know". It works on this Mac without a notes model, in English or Auto, and skips the tone and the Medium edit so the layout stays. If the speech model misspells your name, add it to Vocabulary.
- Style lists the apps you recently dictated into under each context, so you can see which tone each one gets. T3 Code, Chrome, Brave and other apps outside the lists use Other apps, which is Formal until you change it.
- Smoother motion. Capsule cards and pills grow out of the pill as they fade in, the dashboard cross-fades between pages, the toast slides back up as it leaves and Style reveals the notes model picker smoothly. With Reduce Motion on, these are plain fades.
- Spoken language lists the languages the selected model understands. Parakeet TDT v3 and Parakeet Ultra now offer all 25 of their European languages, such as Polish, Dutch, Ukrainian and Greek, instead of a fixed list of 11 that included five they cannot recognize (Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Chinese and Arabic). A language you chose earlier that the model does not support stays visible and is marked as not supported.
- `BetterWisprCLI --bench MANIFEST.jsonl` transcribes a list of clips with one loaded model and prints each transcript with its recognition time, optionally after the app's Light or Medium cleanup or with a `--vocabulary` list for the phrase booster. `scripts/evaluate_transcripts.py --group language` scores each language separately, and `scripts/fetch_bench_corpus.py` downloads a small LibriSpeech and Multilingual LibriSpeech test set to run them on, or with `--accents` conversational Indian, Nigerian and US English from EdAcc.

### Changed
- The dashboard opens on a new Home page. It shows your shortcut and a button to start dictating, your words, speaking time, pace and streak, your most recent dictations, and whether your speech model, microphone and pasting are ready, each one a click away from fixing it. The sidebar groups pages under Personalize and App.
- Settings is laid out as cards (Shortcut, Microphone and language, After you speak, App, Permissions and Voice commands), and each option has a short line explaining what it does.
- Style shows what each cleanup level does to a sample sentence as you switch levels. Each app context has its own card with an icon, its tone choices and a preview of the tone, and the apps it covers are listed underneath.
- While a dictation is being transcribed, the pill shows a spinner instead of the moving bars.
- The notetaker window is calmer. The date and speech model share one line under the title, the tabs are plain text with an underline that slides to the open tab, and the transcript tools sit on the page instead of in a gray box. While recording, the bottom of the window is one bar with the timer, live Me and Them waveforms, a microphone menu and a red Stop button, with the consent reminder underneath. Before the first words arrive, the transcript says it is listening instead of showing an empty box.
- Recording waveforms in the pill, the notetaker and Home now scroll from right to left with your voice. Each bar is a moment of sound, louder syllables stand taller and pauses drop flat, instead of a fixed shape that wobbled at one height.
- Automatic microphone follows Sound settings again, so AirPods and other Bluetooth headsets are used when macOS picks them, with the lid open or closed. A chosen microphone that is disconnected now falls back to the same macOS default instead of skipping a connected headset. Recording from a Bluetooth headset's microphone still drops its playback to call quality until you stop; choose your Mac's own microphone in Settings to avoid that.

### Fixed
- A new version no longer arrives silently. When BetterWispr finds an update while you are working in another app, a toast offers Update and the menu bar shows "Install BetterWispr" with the new version, instead of Sparkle's window opening behind your other windows. With automatic installs on, a downloaded update shows a Restart toast and menu item, because the update otherwise waited until you quit BetterWispr.
- With Spoken language set to Detect automatically and words in Vocabulary, Parakeet no longer rewrites other languages into your vocabulary terms. The phrase booster understands only English, and it turned German "meine" into "Mike". It now runs automatically only when the transcript reads as English.
- The Parakeet phrase booster no longer swaps everyday words for your vocabulary. It turned "not like" into "Notion" and "Thank" into "Thai", and on 300 recorded conversation clips with 20 unspoken terms it changed 44 of them and raised the error rate from 16.2% to 17.4%. A word must now sound closer to a listed term before it is replaced, which changed 5 of those clips. It also no longer drops the word next to a term, as when "seven Harry Potter movies" became "Harry Potter movies".
- Light cleanup resolves a correction after a lone "sorry" that restarts on in, at, on, from, near, into, by or with. "I live in Ahmedabad sorry in Delhi" becomes "I live in Delhi" and "meet at 5 sorry at 6" becomes "meet at 6". Apologies such as "sorry for the wait", "so sorry in advance" and "sorry, the train was late" stay.
- Cleanup no longer drops a repeated number word as a stutter, so "twenty twenty five" and "fifty fifty" keep both words.
- Meeting notes no longer lag while recording. The Me and Them meters used to redraw the whole notetaker window and the floating pill on every audio buffer, about 140 times a second. Now the levels update at most 30 times a second and only the meters and the clock redraw.
- The pill no longer keeps spinning for about three quarters of a second after your words are pasted, and the next dictation can start right away. BetterWispr used to wait in place before putting your previous clipboard back. It now restores the clipboard in the background, and when you dictate again before that happens, the clipboard you had before the first dictation is the one that comes back.
- Tapping the notetaker pill while a meeting is still starting no longer silently cancels it. A tap stops a meeting only once it is recording; Cancel in the notetaker window, the pill's menu and the menu bar still cancel a meeting that is starting.
- Meeting notes no longer wait up to 30 seconds to show what you said, and no longer cut your sentences in half. BetterWispr ended a microphone chunk at a pause only when the level fell below a fixed threshold, which steady room or AirPods noise never did, so chunks ran to 30 seconds. A pause now counts when the level falls back to the background noise of the last 10 seconds, which also ignores the short silent gaps AirPods leave in their signal, and chunks are 3 to 20 seconds long.
- Meeting transcripts no longer repeat the other person's words under Me when your microphone hears them through your speakers. Repeated audio used to be hidden only when a whole Me entry matched them. Now the matching words are removed and what you said around them stays, so "Sure. We should ship the new flow before the end of the month. I agree completely." becomes "Sure. I agree completely." when the other person said the middle sentence at the same time. A Me entry with fewer than three of your own words left is hidden. Show repeated microphone audio still shows the original.
- The other person's lines in a meeting transcript now sit at the right time next to yours. BetterWispr timed their audio by counting what it received, but macOS only sends it while something plays, so their entries were placed seconds early (3.7 seconds in a test) and some of their words picked up by your microphone were not removed from your entries. Each chunk now takes the time its audio was captured.
- Meeting notes start recording as soon as you start them, while the speech model loads. The first words of a meeting used to be lost and the first entry appeared 3 and 11 seconds after the line ended in two tests. What you record while the model loads is transcribed once it is ready, and in testing the first entry appeared about 1 second after the line.
- The other person's last line now appears about a second after they stop talking. macOS sends their audio only while something plays, so when their app went quiet the line waited for the next sound, 6.2 seconds once in a test.
- Repeated microphone audio is also removed when the two transcripts spell a word differently, such as "campaign" and "campage", and a repeated sentence no longer leaves its first word behind in your entry. Words the speech model invents from the repeated audio, rather than repeats, can still remain.

### Fixed
- Videos and music in AirPods and other Bluetooth headphones keep their full sound quality while you dictate. Recording from a Bluetooth headset's microphone switches its playback to call quality, so Automatic now uses your Mac's built-in microphone instead while the lid is open. With the lid closed, or on a Mac without a microphone, Automatic follows Sound settings. If you chose your headphones in Settings, they are still used, so switch Microphone to Automatic to keep full sound quality.

- Dictation and meeting notes wait for microphone audio before reporting readiness and finish the in-flight audio buffer before transcription. Bluetooth startup runs off the UI thread, and a running input queue handles Bluetooth rate changes without a second restart.

- Changing the microphone in Settings also applies to a dictation already in progress. Pending device changes are cancelled when recording stops or is cancelled.
